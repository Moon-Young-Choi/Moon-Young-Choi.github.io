01 / Theory

Statistical experiment

The sample space, inferential unit, nulls, alternatives and parameter boundary.

Inferential unit. The recording cluster, not an individual frame. Labels move only with the complete recording object inside pre-fixed strata or pairs.

Exact null. The full analysis object is invariant under the allowed transformation group; covariance equality alone is weaker.

Alternative. At least one pre-fixed frequency block has a positive leading pooled-whitened population contrast.

Decision. The registered scan returns one global rejection. Localization remains descriptive rather than a support-recovery guarantee.

02 / Theory

Waveform to covariance spike

A conditional log-power expansion isolates the directional covariance term and keeps every remainder visible.

Assumption 2.1. Smooth conditional response

assumption

For u0 = E[U], V = U - u0 and tau^2 = E[V^2], every conditional log-power response g_k is twice continuously differentiable on an interval containing the support of U, with sup |g''_k| <= L_k, and E[V^4] is finite.

Interpretation. The loading ell = g'(u0) and a finite fourth-moment remainder bound are well-defined. Scope. Smoothness and the fourth moment must be justified or calibrated for the intended acoustic regime.

Proposition 2.2. Rank-one approximation of log-power covariance

proposition

Under Assumption 2.1, the current-minus-baseline feature covariance decomposes as tau^2 ell ell^T + E, and the operator norm of E is bounded by conditional-residual drift plus the cross and quadratic Taylor remainders.

Uses. Assumption 2.1

Proof. Expand g(U) at u0 and center the Taylor remainder. Use E[xi | U] = 0 to remove residual cross-covariances. Bound E[V r-bar], Cov(r-bar), and rank-one cross terms by Cauchy-Schwarz and the operator norm identity for outer products. □

Consequently. The acoustic model supplies a quantified rank-one leading covariance term rather than an exact factor claim. Scope. If the remainder bound is of the same order as tau^2 ||ell||^2, a rank-one interpretation is not warranted.

Proposition 2.3. Directional spike survives model mismatch

proposition

If the baseline-whitened block contrast is vartheta u u^T + E_B with ||E_B||op <= eta, its leading relative-covariance eigenvalue is at least 1 + vartheta - eta. When vartheta > eta, the pooled-whitened effect has the stated strictly positive lower bound.

Uses. Positive-definite baseline covariance · Unit loading direction · Operator-norm mismatch bound · Positive signal margin vartheta > eta for the pooled positive lower bound

Proof. Insert u into the Rayleigh quotient. Apply |u^T E_B u| <= ||E_B||op. Use monotonicity of x/(1 + pi_1 x) to translate the baseline-relative gap into the pooled-whitened effect. □

Consequently. The conservative effective signal is (vartheta - eta)+, mapped monotonically to the pooled parameter. Scope. The result supplies no positive-signal guarantee when eta >= vartheta.

03 / Theory

Exchangeability is the null

Exactness is attached to the full-law or randomized-design null at recording-cluster level, never covariance equality alone.

Assumption 3.1. Stratified cluster exchangeability

assumption

Conditional on observed strata and pre-fixed folds, the complete cluster-level data object has the same distribution under every label-preserving transformation in the allowed group.

Interpretation. The cluster is the inferential unit for exact randomization. Scope. Cluster length, sensor, deployment and observed covariates must not create label-dependent laws within a stratum.

Proposition 3.2. Covariance equality is not exchangeability

proposition

Outside a common Gaussian law, equal covariance does not imply invariance under label permutation; even Gaussian groups with different means can share covariance while violating cluster exchangeability.

Proof. Choose two laws with identical covariance but different means or fourth moments. Their joint law changes after labels are swapped. Re-centering changes the statistic but cannot create invariance of the raw data law. □

Consequently. Finite-sample exactness applies to the full-law or design-based null, not a covariance-only null. Scope. A covariance-only null requires a separately proved studentized asymptotic or bootstrap procedure.

Proposition 3.3. Approximate level with guard gaps

proposition

For a stationary beta-mixing process split into N equal clusters separated by guard gap g, Berbee coupling bounds rejection probability by alpha + (N - 1) beta(g), provided the independent-copy randomization test has level alpha.

Uses. Stationarity within stratum · Absolute regularity · Equal cluster lengths · A usable external bound on beta(g) · Assumption 3.1

Proof. Couple clusters sequentially to independent replicas. A union bound limits any coupling mismatch to (N - 1) beta(g). On the no-mismatch event, the dependent-data decision equals the exact independent-copy decision. □

Consequently. Dependence costs an explicit additive level error. Scope. A visually small sample autocorrelation is not evidence that beta(g) equals zero.

04 / Theory

Pooled-whitened scan

The statistic is a monotone Roy-root representation normalized by a concentration-derived block scale.

Definition 4.1. Block PWR matrix and local statistic

definition

For each candidate block, whiten the sample covariance difference by the pooled sample covariance and take its largest eigenvalue.

Uses. Invertible pooled covariance on every candidate block

Interpretation. Large T_B is evidence for a positive current-state covariance component. Scope. A data-selected ridge is outside the primary theorem unless the full selection rule is included and re-proved.

Proposition 4.2. Exact monotone correspondence with Roy's root

proposition

If lambda_k are the generalized eigenvalues of the current and baseline covariance pair, the PWR eigenvalues are h(lambda_k) = (lambda_k - 1)/(pi_0 + pi_1 lambda_k), and h is strictly increasing.

Uses. Positive-definite baseline covariance · Definition 4.1

Proof. Use the same generalized eigenvector for the difference/pooled pair. Symmetric whitening preserves generalized eigenvalues. Differentiate h to obtain a strictly positive derivative. □

Consequently. Within a block, PWR and Fisher-Roy orderings and one-sided rejection regions coincide exactly. Scope. The pooled representation is a reparameterization, not a claim of a new empirical root.

Remark 4.3. Re-expression, not a new empirical statistic

remark

Pooled whitening is selected to keep the whitening matrix fixed across permutations and expose the power analysis; it does not discard Roy-root information.

Uses. Proposition 4.2

Interpretation. Local rank and one-sided rejection order are unchanged. Scope. This equivalence is block-local and does not remove the need to calibrate the global maximum.

Definition 4.4. Theory-derived block scale

definition

Each block is normalized by the effective-sample scale containing direction cost, weighted search cost, and the second-order matrix-concentration term.

Uses. Pre-fixed weights w_B > 0 with sum at most one · Definition 4.1

Interpretation. Different block widths become comparable on a common theoretical noise scale. Scope. Weights are complexity allocations, not posterior signal probabilities, and cannot be tuned after viewing the test data.

Definition 4.5. One-layer PWR-Scan

definition

The global statistic is the maximum of T_B/a_B over the pre-fixed candidate family, calibrated once by the permutation distribution of that complete maximum.

Uses. Candidate family, weights, preprocessing and numerical rules fixed before analysis · Definition 4.1 · Definition 4.4

Interpretation. The method performs one global upper-tail rank calculation per analysis. Scope. Data-dependent candidate additions must be repeated inside every permutation or treated as a new procedure.

05 / Theory

Finite-sample exactness

A finite-group orbit rank controls the entire block maximum at once.

Assumption 5.1. Full exchangeability

assumption

Under the global exchangeability null, the complete data object is distributionally invariant under every element of the allowed finite transformation group.

Interpretation. Every orbit position is probabilistically interchangeable under the null. Scope. Equality of covariance alone does not imply this condition.

Remark 5.2. Two null models have different roles

remark

In the common Gaussian model, equality in law and equality of covariance align, allowing exactness and minimax analysis in one experiment; outside it, exactness requires the stronger full-law null.

Uses. Proposition 3.2 · Assumption 5.1

Interpretation. Finite-sample exactness and covariance-only inference must not be conflated. Scope. Studentized asymptotic randomization or bootstrap validity is a separate theorem.

Definition 5.3. Full-permutation PWR p-value

definition

The p-value is the proportion of distinct orbit assignments whose complete global statistic is at least as large as the observed statistic; ties use the conservative greater-than-or-equal rule.

Uses. Finite orbit of allowed assignments · Definition 4.5

Interpretation. Reject at level alpha exactly when the orbit p-value is at most alpha. Scope. The p-value grid cannot resolve values below one over the orbit size.

Lemma 5.4. Randomization rank lemma

lemma

For a finite group acting on an invariant random object, the upper orbit rank of any measurable statistic is super-uniform.

Uses. Finite group action · Distributional invariance · Assumption 5.1

Proof. Condition on the orbit. Invariance makes the observed orbit position uniform. At most a u fraction of orbit positions can have conservative upper rank at most u; stabilizers and ties only increase ranks. □

Consequently. P(p_S <= u) <= u for every u in [0,1]. Scope. The lemma proves validity, not power or sharpness of the random critical value.

Theorem 5.5. Finite-sample global level

theorem

Under full exchangeability and identical application of the complete analysis function to every orbit element, PWR-Scan controls the global rejection probability at alpha for any sample size, dimension, candidate family and dependence among blocks.

Uses. Assumption 5.1 · Same complete measurable analysis on every assignment · Definition 4.5 · Definition 5.3 · Lemma 5.4

Proof. Treat the already-maximized scan as one measurable statistic. Apply Lemma 5.4 to its orbit rank. No blockwise union bound is needed because maximization is inside S. □

Consequently. The global test is finite-sample level alpha under the explicit exchangeability null. Scope. Exactness does not extend automatically to unequal full laws with equal covariance.

Corollary 5.6. Gaussian finite-sample exactness

corollary

Independent samples from one common zero-mean Gaussian distribution satisfy Theorem 5.5 whenever every pooled block covariance is invertible; no asymptotic ratio condition is required for validity.

Uses. Common Gaussian law · Invertible pooled covariance on every block · Theorem 5.5

Interpretation. The Gaussian reference experiment inherits finite-sample global level control. Scope. Exact size alpha additionally depends on a continuous orbit statistic and alignment of alpha with the orbit grid.

06 / Theory

Monte Carlo randomization

The plus-one correction preserves validity and an exact binomial expression prices finite permutation budgets.

Definition 5.7. Monte Carlo PWR p-value

definition

With R independent uniform transformations, the randomized p-value adds the observed assignment as one exceedance and divides by R + 1.

Uses. Transformations sampled independently of the data and uniformly from the allowed group · Definition 5.3

Interpretation. The smallest possible Monte Carlo p-value is 1/(R + 1), never zero. Scope. Removing either plus one invalidates the finite-sample calibration.

Theorem 5.8. Finite-sample validity of random permutations

theorem

Under full exchangeability, independent uniform random transformations with the plus-one correction produce a super-uniform p-value and hence a level-alpha test.

Uses. Assumption 5.1 · Independent uniform transformations · Definition 5.7 · Lemma 5.4

Proof. Introduce an independent uniform reference group element G0. Conditional on the orbit, the R + 1 transformed statistics are exchangeable. The conservative upper rank of the reference statistic is super-uniform; relative transformations recover the implemented p-value law. □

Consequently. Monte Carlo randomization retains finite-sample validity for every R. Scope. Validity can coexist with no rejection resolution when R + 1 < 1/alpha.

Remark 5.9. The plus-one correction is mandatory

remark

Zero observed exceedances do not justify p = 0; rejection at level alpha requires R + 1 >= 1/alpha, and the candidate count does not multiply this one-layer resolution requirement.

Uses. Definition 5.7 · Theorem 5.8

Interpretation. Randomization resolution is explicit and auditable. Scope. Increasing the candidate family still affects the statistic's distribution even though it does not multiply the p-value grid requirement.

Proposition 5.10. Monte Carlo power transfer

proposition

If the full-orbit p-value is at most gamma < alpha with probability at least 1 - beta, Monte Carlo power is at least one minus beta minus the explicit binomial tail for crossing alpha(R + 1).

Uses. A full-orbit p-value margin gamma < alpha · Definition 5.3 · Definition 5.7

Proof. Conditional on data, the exceedance count is Binomial(R,p(D)). On p(D) <= gamma it is stochastically dominated by Binomial(R,gamma). Union-bound failure of the full-orbit margin and the binomial resolution event. □

Consequently. R can be selected from a target additional power loss, not by an informal 'large enough' rule. Scope. The proposition transfers an established full-orbit margin; it does not create that margin.

07 / Theory

Observed and permuted tails

Gaussian covariance concentration and hypergeometric signal erasure connect the real permutation threshold to the analytic scale.

Assumption 6.1. Balanced Gaussian two-sample model

assumption

The two independent samples are centered Gaussian, both group proportions stay in a fixed interval away from zero, every baseline block covariance is positive definite, and each current-to-baseline relative covariance is uniformly bounded above and below.

Interpretation. All rate constants may depend on the fixed balance and relative-conditioning bounds, but not on n, p or the block family. Scope. The condition does not require a well-conditioned raw baseline covariance; it constrains the relative change after whitening.

Assumption 6.2. Moderate-dimensional range

assumption

For every candidate block and each tail parameter used by the proof, dimension plus weighted entropy plus tail size is no larger than a sufficiently small constant times the effective sample size.

Uses. Assumption 6.1 · Definition 4.4

Interpretation. Second-order covariance concentration remains controlled uniformly over the candidate family. Scope. The proportional b/n regime requires separate random-matrix asymptotics and is not covered.

Lemma 6.3. Local concentration of the observed Gaussian contrast

lemma

On a fixed block, the observed leading PWR root falls below the population root by more than a constant times r_ne(b,t+1) with probability at most 2e^{-t}.

Uses. Assumption 6.1 · Assumption 6.2 · Definition 4.1 · Lemma B.1 · Lemma B.2

Proof. Move to population pooled coordinates. Concentrate both group covariance errors in operator norm. Control inverse-square-root perturbation of the sample whitener. Apply Weyl's inequality to the largest eigenvalue. □

Consequently. The true block score has an explicit high-probability signal lower bound. Scope. Constants rely on Gaussianity, group balance and bounded relative covariance.

Lemma 6.4. Gaussian random-partition maximum scan

lemma

For a uniform label reassignment independent of the data, the normalized maximum scan exceeds a constant multiple of its tail inflation factor with joint probability at most 2e^{-t}, even under the bounded Gaussian alternative.

Uses. Assumption 6.1 · Assumption 6.2 · Uniform random assignment independent of data · Definition 4.5 · Lemma B.2 · Lemma B.3

Proof. Express the permuted covariance difference exactly as a centered finite-population matrix sum. Control hypergeometric imbalance of original group membership. Condition on the assignment and concentrate non-identically distributed Gaussian quadratic forms over a net. Transfer population to sample pooled whitening and use the weighted union bound over overlapping blocks. □

Consequently. Random partition erases the O(tau) signal up to hypergeometric leakage and matrix fluctuation on the same a_B scale. Scope. The lemma is a joint data-permutation tail, not a pointwise deterministic bound for every conditional permutation quantile.

Remark 6.5. The new proof ingredient

remark

The nonstandard step is simultaneous control of hypergeometric signal leakage and matrix fluctuation after repartitioning two different Gaussian populations, uniformly over overlapping generalized-eigenvalue blocks.

Uses. Lemma 6.4

Interpretation. Lemma 6.4 closes the gap between the actual permutation threshold and the analytic minimax scale. Scope. Generic permutation-power results do not automatically imply this nonsmooth overlapping-block eigenvalue bound.

08 / Theory

Finite-sample power

A joint data-permutation tail becomes a high-probability conditional orbit bound through Fubini and Markov.

Theorem 6.6. Finite-sample permutation power lower bound

theorem

If one true block's pooled population root exceeds the sum of its observed-contrast concentration radius and the normalized random-partition threshold, the full-orbit PWR test has power at least 1 - beta, subject to orbit resolution.

Uses. Assumption 6.1 · Assumption 6.2 · Positive population effect on a candidate block · Orbit size greater than 1/alpha · Lemma 6.3 · Lemma 6.4 · Definition 5.3

Proof. Lemma 6.3 puts the observed score above a fixed threshold with failure beta/2. Use Lemma 6.4 and Fubini to bound the expected non-identity conditional orbit tail. Markov's inequality converts the joint tail into a high-probability conditional tail. Combine the identity mass and residual orbit budget to obtain p <= alpha. □

Consequently. The actual full-permutation test, not a surrogate analytic scan, receives a finite-sample power guarantee. Scope. The constants are non-sharp and the theorem remains inside the balanced Gaussian moderate-dimensional regime.

Remark 6.7. Orbit resolution

remark

The full-permutation p-value cannot be smaller than one over the orbit size, so |G|^{-1} <= alpha is necessary for rejection; in a balanced two-sample orbit this condition becomes automatic quickly as N grows.

Uses. Definition 5.3 · Theorem 6.6

Interpretation. The theorem reserves the usable alpha budget after accounting for the identity mass. Scope. A tiny orbit can make a formally valid test unable to reject.

Remark 6.8. Why the Markov step matters

remark

Power depends on a data-conditional permutation tail, whereas Lemma 6.4 is joint in data and permutation; Markov's inequality is the explicit bridge from the joint expectation to the high-probability conditional bound.

Uses. Lemma 6.4 · Theorem 6.6

Interpretation. An analogy between analytic and permutation thresholds cannot replace this conditioning step. Scope. The conversion is conservative and does not yield a sharp conditional quantile approximation.

Corollary 6.9. Moderate-regime detection condition

corollary

For fixed alpha, beta, balance and relative-conditioning constants, uniform moderate dimension and sufficient orbit resolution reduce Theorem 6.6 to the square-root direction plus square-root search rate.

Uses. Fixed alpha and beta · Uniform Assumption 6.2 · b + ell = o(n_e) · Adequate orbit resolution · Theorem 6.6 · Definition 4.4

Interpretation. A sufficiently large constant times sqrt(b/n_e) + sqrt(ell/n_e) yields power at least 1 - beta. Scope. This is a rate statement; it does not supply a sharp finite-sample constant.

Corollary 6.10. Finite Monte Carlo power

corollary

Replacing alpha by a strict margin gamma in Theorem 6.6 and applying Proposition 5.10 yields full finite-sample Monte Carlo power minus an explicit binomial tail.

Uses. Orbit resolution below gamma · Theorem 6.6 separation at the gamma margin · Theorem 6.6 · Proposition 5.10

Interpretation. The permutation budget R can be tied to a stated power-loss tolerance. Scope. The locked V2 campaign needed to estimate operational constants remains pending.

09 / Theory

Minimax lower bound

Spherical direction and disjoint-location mixtures separately expose the two unavoidable search costs.

Theorem 6.11. Minimax lower bound for unknown direction and location

theorem

Even when the baseline covariance is known and only the current Gaussian sample must be tested, total risk stays bounded away from zero when theta is below a constant times max{sqrt(b/n_1), sqrt(log M/n_1)} over M disjoint candidate blocks.

Uses. Gaussian oracle experiment · M pairwise-disjoint blocks of width b · Rank-one positive spike in the small-signal range theta < 1 · Appendix B.11.1 · Appendix B.11.2

Proof. Unknown direction: mix the spike direction uniformly on the b-sphere and bound chi-square divergence when n theta^2/b is small. Unknown location: mix fixed orthogonal directions over M disjoint blocks and bound divergence when n theta^2 <= c log M. Use Le Cam's inequality in each subexperiment and take the harder of the two costs. □

Consequently. No test uniformly beats both the direction and location square-root costs. Scope. The theorem is a rate lower bound with universal constants, not a sharp power envelope.

10 / Theory

Matched minimax rate

The permutation-calibrated upper rate and Gaussian information lower rate agree to constants.

Theorem 6.12. Balanced Gaussian minimax rate

theorem

With comparable group sizes, M equal-width candidates weighted 1/M, moderate dimension and a disjoint packing, the actual full-permutation PWR-Scan attains the minimax rank-one separation rate sqrt(b/n) + sqrt(log M/n), up to constant factors.

Uses. Assumption 6.1 · b + log M = o(n) · Equal weights · Candidate family contains the lower-bound packing · Adequate orbit resolution · Corollary 6.9 · Theorem 6.11 · Proposition 4.2

Proof. Balance makes n_e, n and n_1 equivalent up to constants. The monotone pooled effect is equivalent to the bounded baseline spike. Corollary 6.9 supplies the upper rate; Theorem 6.11 supplies max of the same two terms. Use max{x,y} <= x+y <= 2 max{x,y}. □

Consequently. The same permutation-calibrated statistic that is exact under the null is rate-optimal in the Gaussian reference experiment. Scope. No uniformly most-powerful, sharp-constant, proportional-dimensional or non-Gaussian optimality claim is made.

Remark 6.13. What rate optimality does not say

remark

Theorem 6.12 establishes order and fixed-factor equivalence only; it does not imply uniform most-powerfulness, a sharp asymptotic constant, a power envelope, or optimality beyond independent balanced Gaussian moderate dimension.

Uses. Theorem 6.12

Interpretation. The public claim is deliberately narrower than 'optimal in all settings'. Scope. Real-audio performance and external validity require empirical evidence, not extrapolation from this theorem.

11 / Theory

Model mismatch

Operator-norm error subtracts explicitly from usable signal; it is not hidden in a robustness slogan.

Assumption 6.14. Approximate block spike

assumption

On some candidate block, the population pooled contrast equals tau u u^T plus a symmetric remainder R whose operator norm is at most eta.

Uses. Unit direction · Operator-norm remainder bound · Proposition 2.2 · Proposition 2.3

Interpretation. Model mismatch enters the power theorem through a single conservative signal erosion eta. Scope. Eta must be bounded externally; describing leakage as merely 'small' is not sufficient.

Theorem 6.15. Mismatch-robust permutation power

theorem

Under the Gaussian and moderate-dimensional assumptions plus an approximate spike, Theorem 6.6 remains valid after replacing the nominal signal by (tau - eta)+.

Uses. Assumption 6.1 · Assumption 6.2 · Assumption 6.14 · Theorem 6.6 · Proposition B.9

Proof. Weyl's inequality lower-bounds the population leading eigenvalue by tau - eta. All subsequent concentration and random-partition arguments use only that population lower bound. □

Consequently. The finite-sample power guarantee degrades explicitly with operator-norm mismatch. Scope. When eta >= tau the theorem guarantees no positive signal and no favorable power claim is justified.

12 / Theory

Adaptive multiscale scan

A shifted geometric cover adapts over unknown interval width with an explicit scale-search cost.

Theorem 7.1. Deterministic interval covering

theorem

Every admissible contiguous interval is contained in a member of the shifted geometric candidate family whose length is less than 2(1 + epsilon) times the true interval length.

Uses. Geometric widths · Grid spacing at most epsilon times the scale width · Pre-fixed minimum and maximum widths

Proof. Choose the smallest geometric scale at least as wide as the target interval, making it less than twice the target width. Choose the last shifted grid start not exceeding the target start. The start mismatch is below one grid step, so the enlarged candidate covers the target while its length obeys the stated factor. □

Consequently. An O(p)-scale deterministic family covers every interval without data-dependent screening. Scope. A single aligned dyadic tree does not provide this uniform constant-factor covering for intervals crossing a tree boundary.

Lemma 7.2. PSD signal monotonicity under supersets

lemma

If a positive-semidefinite covariance increase is supported inside I x I and I is contained in B, the leading pooled-whitened population root on B is at least its value on I.

Uses. Positive-semidefinite covariance increase · Support contained in the true interval · Definition 4.1

Proof. Write the leading root as a generalized Rayleigh maximum on B. Restrict candidate vectors to zero outside I. On this subset, numerator and denominator equal the I-block Rayleigh quotient. □

Consequently. Covering enlarges noise dimension but does not attenuate a strict PSD block signal. Scope. An indefinite remainder must be handled separately through the eta mismatch bound.

Theorem 7.3. Adaptive power of multiscale PWR-Scan

theorem

Uniformly over admissible widths, a PSD interval signal is detected with power at least 1 - beta when its population PWR effect exceeds the direction, location, scale-adaptation and second-order concentration terms induced by the shifted geometric family.

Uses. Assumption 6.1 · Uniform assumption-6-2 · PSD interval-supported signal · Adequate orbit resolution · Theorem 7.1 · Lemma 7.2 · Theorem 6.6

Proof. Select the constant-factor covering block from Theorem 7.1. Use Lemma 7.2 to preserve the population effect. Bound covering-block dimension and its weighted entropy by b and Lambda_b. Insert those bounds into Theorem 6.6 and absorb constants. □

Consequently. One permutation-calibrated multiscale maximum adapts over unknown interval width. Scope. The theorem is an adaptive upper bound and does not prove that its log-log scale cost is information-theoretically necessary.

Corollary 7.4. Near-minimax width adaptation

corollary

For fixed design constants and moderate dimension, multiscale PWR-Scan detects at the fixed-width direction-plus-location rate with an additional log-log term for unknown scale.

Uses. Fixed alpha, beta and epsilon · Adequate orbit resolution · b + Lambda_b = o(n) · Theorem 7.3 · Theorem 6.11

Interpretation. Width adaptation costs at most a logarithm of the number of scales inside the square-root entropy term. Scope. Removal or necessity of the adaptation term is an unresolved sharp-adaptation question.

Lemma B.1. Gaussian covariance concentration on one block

lemma

For m independent standard b-variate Gaussian vectors, the sample covariance differs from identity in operator norm by at most 2r + r^2 with probability at least 1 - 2e^{-t^2/2}, where r = sqrt(b/m) + t/sqrt(m).

Uses. Independent standard Gaussian vectors

Proof. Represent the covariance as m^{-1}G^T G. Apply the extreme singular-value inequality to G. Square the normalized singular-value interval and take its largest deviation from one. □

Consequently. A nonasymptotic operator-norm radius is available block by block. Scope. The standard statement assumes Gaussian observations and no data-dependent dimension choice.

Lemma B.2. Average of non-identical Gaussian covariances

lemma

Independent centered Gaussian vectors with uniformly bounded covariance eigenvalues obey the same sqrt((b+x)/m) + (b+x)/m relative operator-norm concentration around their average covariance.

Uses. Independent centered Gaussian vectors · Uniform upper and lower covariance bounds · Lemma B.1

Proof. For each fixed direction, centered Gaussian squares are uniformly sub-exponential. Apply scalar Bernstein on a one-quarter net. Lift the net maximum to operator norm. □

Consequently. Permutation groups containing observations from both original populations remain concentrable after conditioning on membership. Scope. Constants depend on the covariance condition ratio.

Lemma B.3. Weighted union bound

lemma

If block event failures are bounded by c e^{-x}, evaluating block B at x + log(1/w_B) and summing weights no larger than one yields simultaneous success with probability at least 1 - c e^{-x}.

Uses. Positive pre-fixed weights summing to at most one

Proof. Substitute the block-specific tail allocation. Sum c e^{-x} w_B over blocks. □
